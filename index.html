Research

Research Focus and Scholarly Work

My research interests are centered on transportation safety, intelligent transportation systems, traffic operations, and the use of computer vision and data-driven methods to better understand road-user behavior.

My undergraduate research (thesis) focused on safety analysis at heterogeneous, non-lane-based traffic intersections in Dhaka, Bangladesh. I worked with naturalistic traffic video collected from five intersections and developed a computer vision pipeline for detecting and tracking different road users, transforming their movements into real-world trajectories, and evaluating traffic conflicts using surrogate safety measures such as Time-to-Collision (TTC), Post-Encroachment Time (PET), and Deceleration Rate to Avoid Collision (DRAC). The work examined how conflict frequency and severity vary across signalized, manually controlled, and uncontrolled intersections.

Through this work, I became particularly interested in the challenges of studying mixed traffic environments where conventional lane-based assumptions do not always hold. I am interested in how computer vision, trajectory data, simulation, and statistical or machine learning methods can be combined to better understand traffic interactions and support more proactive approaches to road safety.

Looking ahead, I would like to explore broader problems in transportation engineering, including traffic safety, traffic flow and operations, intelligent transportation systems, connected and automated mobility, travel behavior, travel demand modeling, transportation data analytics, and the application of AI and machine learning in transportation. I am especially interested in research that connects methodological development with real-world transportation problems.

Signalized Intersection Design and Simulation in PTV VISSIM

Designed and simulated a four-phase, four-leg signalized intersection for heterogeneous mixed traffic using PTV VISSIM. The project involved developing the intersection geometry, defining traffic inputs and turning movements, setting up signal control, and evaluating operational performance through measures such as queue length and vehicle delay. Based on the simulation outputs, signal timings were adjusted and optimized to improve traffic progression, reduce excessive queuing, and achieve a more balanced operation across the approaches. The project provided hands-on experience in linking intersection design decisions with microsimulation-based performance evaluation.

03
Academic ProjectFeatured

Flexible Pavement Design Using IITPAVE

Designed a flexible pavement structure following IRC:37-2018 using a mechanistic-empirical design approach. The work included calculating Vehicle Damage Factors, estimating cumulative design traffic in equivalent standard axle loads, determining allowable strain limits, and modeling the pavement as a multilayer elastic system in IITPAVE. Layer thicknesses were refined through several iterations by checking both subgrade rutting and bituminous fatigue cracking criteria. The final economical section consisted of a 150 mm bituminous layer, 120 mm granular base, and 140 mm granular subbase, with both critical strain requirements satisfied.

Environmental Impact Assessment of the Jamuna Railway Bridge Construction Project

Prepared a comprehensive academic Environmental Impact Assessment for the Jamuna Railway Bridge project, a major 4.8 km dual-gauge, double-track railway crossing over the Jamuna River. The assessment reviewed the project’s physical, biological, and socio-economic baseline conditions and evaluated potential impacts during pre-construction, construction, and operation. Pedestrian Truss Bridge Design Using CSI SAP2000

This was an individual academic project completed as part of the CEE 4712 Structural Analysis and Design Sessional II course. A full 3D pedestrian truss bridge model was developed in SAP2000 using A992Fy50 steel, with nine distinct member types including chords, diagonals, floor beams, stringers, bracings, and portal frames. Dead and factored pedestrian live loads were applied, and an iterative trial and error process was carried out to revise and finalize member sections until all members passed the stress and capacity check. The final design confirmed expected truss behavior, with bending moments confined to floor beams and stringers while main truss members carried pure axial forces.

Structural Analysis and Design of a Steel Truss Bridge Using SAP2000

This was an individual academic project completed as part of the CEE 4614 Structural Analysis and Design Sessional 1 course. The project involved the structural analysis and design of an 11-meter steel truss bridge intended for an agricultural setting in Chittagong, a region characterized by significant wind exposure. A detailed 3D model was developed in SAP2000 with realistic support conditions and member properties. Load analysis covered dead loads including self-weight, roofing, and purlins, as well as live loads and wind loads calculated based on Chittagong's specified wind speed (80 m/s) and exposure category following BNBC 2020 provisions. Both windward and leeward wind effects were considered in the analysis. Member forces obtained from the analysis were used to size and optimize the truss members for structural safety while keeping the design practical and economical.
